    Introduction

    BiG is a web platform that runs the operational side of a festival, from the first ticket sale to the last volunteer shift.

    Why this guide exists

    This guide explains what the platform does today, how each workflow runs from start to finish, where its current limits are, and what's coming next. It's written for organisers, directors, partners and anyone deciding how the festival should run, not for developers.

    How the pieces connect Interactive

    Everything in BiG belongs to one of two families. Festival records (events, passes, bookings, tents) start fresh with each edition. People records (users, departments, attendance) are shared across every event, so a volunteer's history carries over from one year to the next. Each event keeps a staff list of the people who work it. Hospitality's mess plan, guests, meal passes and beds belong to an event too, while the staff they feed and house are people records.     Access tiers: Read, Write, Manage Interactive

    Besides their home department, anyone can be given access to other departments, at one of three tiers. Each tier includes the ones below it. Access is given by department key: departments that share a key share the same access, so one grant covers all of them, including ones added later. See Departments.

    See the department's pages and information.

    Write

    Also make changes and carry out day-to-day actions.

    Manage

    Also oversee the department and the people in it.

    Team Leads manage automatically

    A Team Lead always has Manage access to their own department(s), and to any department that shares its key. Nobody has to remember to grant it, and it can't drift out of sync.

    Example: a volunteer helping out at Box Office

    A volunteer whose home department is Operations is needed at the gate for the weekend. An admin gives them Read access to Box Office. They can now open Bookings and Registration without moving departments, and their attendance stays with Operations. Removing the grant afterwards takes them back to normal.

    Each event's staff list

    Roles, departments and access are the same for every event. What changes from one event to the next is its staff list: who works that event. Team Leads and volunteers can open only the active events they're on, and only the people on it are counted for that event's meals, beds, attendance, announcements and SOS alerts. Developers, Directors and Admins are always on every event. See Who works each event.

    3

    Dashboard, Billboard, SOS and sign-in

    Signing in takes people to a dashboard of the active event's headline numbers, the Billboard keeps every announcement sent to them, and the SOS page brings participants' calls for help to the right staff.

    Sign in

    Email and password. Repeated failed attempts pause sign-in for a while. Sessions last up to 7 days. If one expires mid-task, the next action returns you to sign-in with a notice, so nobody keeps working on a page that has silently stopped saving.

    Dashboard

    Passes sold, admitted, pending admission and accommodation bookings, plus volunteers by department for admins. Charts below show admitted vs. yet-to-admit across festival passes, tents and vehicles.

    Confirmations for deletes, vacates and overwrites appear as branded in-app dialogs. In every side panel and dialog, Close or Cancel sits on the left and the main action on the right, on phones too. The sidebar footer always shows the current version.

    The sidebar groups (Main, Box Office, Accommodation, Workshops, Hospitality and Admin) open and close with a tap on their title. Main starts open and the others closed. The group holding the page you're on always opens, and BiG remembers your choice in that browser. When Main is closed, the Billboard's unread count shows on its title.

    Billboard Everyone

    Billboard, under Main in the sidebar, keeps every announcement sent to you, pinned up as post-it notes with the newest first. It's the place to catch up on anything you missed, even if you never turned notifications on or have muted them.

    • One colour per type. Each note takes its type's colour (General, Urgent, Schedule, Weather, Reminder, Task, Meals or SOS) and shows the title, the message and when it was sent.
    • A NEW sticker marks a note you haven't opened yet.
    • Tap a note to open it as a large note with the full message, From (who sent it), To (who it went to) and Sent (date and time).
    • The All and Unread tabs switch between every note and only the new ones. Mark all read clears every NEW sticker at once.
    • The newest 100 notes show first. Show older brings in earlier ones.
    • A red number next to Billboard in the sidebar shows how many notes are unread.

    The board fills the page width on a desktop. On phones, notes sit two to a row.

    Where notes come from

    • Announcements sent to you from Admin › Announcements.
    • Emails sent: when a ticket or custom email send you started has finished, a private note from BiG, addressed to "Only you", gives how many were sent and how many failed.
    • Meal alerts from the Hospitality team, to the event's staff.
    • Hospitality tasks for Team Leads: a private Task addressed to "Only you". It either asks you to mark attendance before a meal's kitchen count is due, or lists people from your team who ate without attendance marked. Tasks sent automatically show "From: BiG".
    • SOS alerts: for the people who handle SOS, a private red note for each help request from the public festival page. See SOS.
    • Tapping a BiG notification on your phone or computer opens the Billboard with that note already open.

    Announcements sent before the Billboard was added aren't on it.

    SOS Admins and chosen departments

    Participants can ask the festival team for help from the Emergency button on the public festival page. Each request arrives on the SOS page, under Main in the sidebar, which shows a red count of open requests.

    • Who handles it: Directors, Admins, Developers and members of the departments picked on Admin › Public Page, such as a medical or security team. Only they see SOS in the sidebar.
    • The alert: each request arrives as a notification and as a private red note on their Billboard. It comes through even when notifications are muted. Department members get it only if they're on the staff list of the event shown on the public page.
    • Each request shows when it was sent, a small map, how accurate the location is, Directions, the participant's message and call-back number (tap to call), and Outside the venue when it came from beyond the festival ground.
    • Mark handled, with an optional note, records who handled it and when. Open requests stay at the top.
    • The page refreshes itself every 15 seconds. Tapping the notification opens that request.
    • Locations and phone numbers are deleted automatically 30 days after the event.

    SOS is off until it's switched on in Admin › Public Page. Anyone on the public page can send one, so the page reminds staff to treat it like a phone call and check before acting.

    Venue Map

    The Venue Map shows the festival ground for the active event: the places that matter, the zones and routes between them, what's happening in each workshop area right now, and whether the Mess Hall and the Box Office are open.

    Venue Map, under Main in the sidebar, is open to everyone signed in. It shows a Street map or a Satellite view. Each event has its own map, so a new venue starts clean. On a desktop the map stays in place and the side panel scrolls. On phones the lists sit below the map, and picking an item scrolls back up to it.

    Pointing at a pin, an area or its badge shows a short tooltip. On phones, tapping it shows the same information in a small popup.

    Locations

    A location is a single place on the ground, shown as a coloured pin. Each has a name, a type (Water station, Toilets, Stage, Gate, Entry point, Box Office, Tent field or Other), an optional responsible department and notes. Each event has at most one Box Office pin.

    • Add location, then tap the map, or tap Use my location to drop a pin where you're standing. BiG shows how accurate the phone's position is.
    • Pins are locked every time the map opens, so scrolling on a phone can't move one by accident. People who can edit pins see a lock button next to Street / Satellite. Tap it to unlock, then drag a pin to fine-tune it. A pin open in its form can always be moved. Edit or delete a pin from its card.
    • Type filters with counts, and a list beside the map, help find a place quickly.
    • Directions and Street View open Google Maps at that spot. Go to jumps to pasted coordinates.
    • Each location shows which zone it's in, with a warning if it's outside the festival boundary.

    Areas and lines

    Areas mark zones on the ground and lines mark routes and edges. Each has a name, a kind, a responsible department and notes, and shows its kind's colour and icon on the map.

    Why slots instead of whole days?

    Volunteers often move mid-day: Operations in the morning, Box Office in the afternoon. Recording each slot separately means both departments get credit for the right hours. One department can't silently overwrite another's record: the platform asks first.

    Attendance feeds the kitchen

    Hospitality counts Team Leads and volunteers at a meal only when they're marked present in the slot that meal counts. Marking attendance on time keeps the kitchen's numbers right. See Who's counted at meals.

    History and calendar

    The History tab summarises each member's attendance over a date range. The Calendar tab shows everyone against every date, with each cell marked, unmarked or not applicable (for example before someone's date of joining). It opens scrolled to today.

    Registration: the gate

    The Registration page opens with live numbers: Admitted, Pending Admits, Incomplete Details and Spot Registrations. Staff find a booking by scanning the QR code from its ticket email or the person's wrist band, or by searching by name, phone, email or order number. Every scanned ticket code is checked first: one that has been altered or made up is rejected with a message, and the scanner keeps running. Either way staff land on Booking Found, which lists every attendee on the booking and offers the same email buttons and history as the detail panel.

    Admit, not Check In

    At the gate the step is called Admit. "Check In" is kept for a planned online step, where each participant fills in their forms before arriving through a secure one-time link.

    At the top of Registration, a card shows whether the Box Office is open or closed on the Venue Map, and who set it and when. The Box Office Team Lead, Directors, Admins and Developers switch it with Open / Closed and can add a short note, such as the queue time. If the event has no Box Office pin yet, the card says so and links to the Venue Map.

    Wrist bands

    Every Festival Pass holder gets a wrist band when they're admitted. From then on, the band's QR code identifies them at Registration and at the workshop door.

    1. Tap Admit on the attendee's Festival Pass. The scanner opens as Admit: Scan Wrist Band, with the attendee's name.
    2. Scan the band being handed over.
    3. Tap Admit and link band. The person is admitted and the band becomes theirs, in one step.
    • One band, one person, ever. A band already linked to someone else is refused, naming who has it. Ticket QR codes are refused as bands.
    • Staff bands are recognised. A band that belongs to a staff member shows "This is a staff band" at Registration instead of "unknown band". See Staff wrist bands.
    • Replace Band, with a reason, for a lost or broken band. The old band is flagged as replaced. Wherever it's scanned, a red Replaced band card shows whose it was, why it was replaced, and the person's band now.
    • Link Band appears only for people admitted before bands were in use.
    • A Band badge on each admitted Festival Pass opens its band history: the current band and any replaced ones, who linked or replaced them, and when.

    Anyone who can use Box Office can admit people and link bands. Replacing a band is for admins, Directors, Developers, members of the Box Office department, and anyone with Write or Manage access to Box Office.

    Where band scanning is used

    • Box Office: Admit, Link Band, Replace Band, and the Registration scanner.
    • Workshops: adding people to session lists, and the door. See Workshops.
    • Hospitality: staff bands at the Serving desk. See Staff wrist bands.
    How tent allocation keeps things right
    • Linked to a person: a tent pass must be tied to a festival pass holder. The platform suggests the likely match, even when they bought on a different booking.
    • Right tent type: only tents of the matching tent pass type are offered.
    • Never overfilled: two staff members can't both take the last place in a tent. The platform settles that safely.
    • Gender-aware: mixing genders in a tent needs a deliberate staff confirmation.

    Spot Registration

    For walk-ups with no booking, staff create one on the spot. They choose items from the active event's pass types, enter the buyer's details, and the platform assigns an order number automatically. With Email ticket to buyer ticked (the default, for staff who can send ticket emails), the buyer gets their ticket email straight away. The new booking goes straight to Booking Found and is admitted like any other. Spot registrations are counted separately, so walk-up demand is visible at a glance.

    Accommodation

    Accommodation is where the physical tents are built and kept accurate. Putting people into tents happens at the gate in Box Office. Both work on the same live tent records.

    Inventory Box Office team & admins

    • Search by unit number or occupant name. Filter by tent type and by occupancy. Numbers sort naturally (SO2 before SO10).
    • Rows are colour-coded by how full they are, and expand to show each occupant's details and admission status.
    • Edit a unit's number or capacity. Capacity can't drop below the number of people already in it.
    • Delete a unit only when it's empty.
    • Vacate an occupant directly, after a confirmation.

    Planner Everyone

    Workshops › Planner shows the event's sessions on a calendar: one tab per event day, with the workshop venues as columns. Sessions take their category's colour, and each one shows how many people are on its list and waitlist. On phones the venue columns scroll sideways.

    A session is one run of a workshop at a venue. It has a start and end, when pre-registration opens and closes, a cut-off, a soft list limit, and optional substitute facilitators for that session only. A workshop can run several times, in the same venue or in different ones.

    A session's times always run in this order: registration opens, registration closes, cut-off, start, end. A new session comes filled in with registration opening 1 hour before the start, closing 30 minutes before and the cut-off 15 minutes before, and all of these can be changed. If the cut-off is left empty, it is 15 minutes before the start. If the registration closing time is left empty, registration closes at the cut-off. The form checks the order before saving. Moving the start in the form moves the end, registration and cut-off by the same amount. Dragging a session on the Planner moves its registration and cut-off with it.

    • Planning (the Workshop Team Lead and admins): tap + New session or select empty time to add one. On a phone, tap an empty slot to add a one-hour session there. Drag a session to another venue or time, or drag its bottom edge to change its length.
    • Warnings never block: two sessions in one venue at once, the same workshop twice in a day, a list limit above the venue's capacity, or a session outside the event's dates. The Workshop Team Lead decides.
    • Tapping a session opens a panel with its numbers (on the list, waitlist, walk-ins, attended), venue, facilitators, registration window, cut-off and list limit.
    • Pre-registration and Entry switches (the Workshop team): Open / Closed and Entry open / Entry closed. The lit button is always the real state right now, whether it comes from the times or from staff. Underneath, each says Following the times or Set by hand, with Back to times. While registration is open, its closing time is shown. Next to the switches are Show places left on the map and Open lists & door.
    • Now is always there. It shows the sessions running now, then the rest of today in order, scrolling sideways, with the same status words as the Venue Map. When nothing is left, it says "Nothing else today". Tapping a session opens it.
    • A red line marks the current time across every venue on today's calendar. The Planner refreshes once a minute while it's open.

    Lists and the door Workshop team

    Each session has its own page, with its numbers at the top and two modes: List for pre-registration and Door to let people in. Chips at the top say whether pre-registration is open, with its closing time, and whether entry is open. In both, staff find a person with Scan wrist band, or by searching their name, email, phone or order number. Searching is the way in if scanning isn't working. Each result shows as a large colour-coded card.

    Building the list

    • Only Festival Pass holders already admitted at the gate can be added, and only while pre-registration is open.
    • Once the list limit is reached, people go on the waitlist. When someone leaves the list before the session, the first person waiting moves up automatically, and BiG says who.
    • BiG warns, without blocking, when someone is already on another session of the same workshop or on a session at an overlapping time.

    At the door

    The door follows the times

    Entry opens when registration closes, and people on the list go in from then. From the cut-off, the waitlist and walk-ins go in too. Before registration closes, the door lets nobody in. Staff can close entry at any time, for example when the room is full, and open it again or hand it back to the times.

    🛡

    Built to protect real data

    When several staff work the same booking or tent at once, the platform settles it cleanly. No double allocations, no overfilled tents.

    🎯

    ✅

    🔗

    Fits existing sales channels

    🌐

    Open to participants, safely

    BiG as an installable app

    BiG is an app staff can keep on their phone's home screen: quick to open, still there when the signal drops, and able to send notifications.

    Available now, with more to come

    BiG can be installed on phones and computers, and it sends notifications. Next, the most useful screens will keep working without signal; see When the signal drops.

    Installing BiG

    No app store, no download, no new account. Open BiG in the browser and sign in as usual.

    • Android phones and computers (Chrome): a message, Install BiG, appears. Tap Install.
    • iPhone and iPad: a message explains the steps. Tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

    Either message can be closed for good. Once installed, BiG opens full screen from its own green "BiG" icon. Participants can install the public festival page separately, as BiG Festival; see Public Page.

    What changes for staff

    📲

    On your home screen

    Add BiG from the browser in a few taps. It works on Android phones, iPhones and computers.

    🖥

    Opens like an app

    BiG gets its own icon and opens full screen, without the address bar and tabs. That leaves more room for the gate and attendance screens.

    ⚡

    Quick to start

    The app itself is kept on the phone, so it opens quickly even when the signal is weak.

    📶

    Opens without signal

    BiG still opens when the connection drops, and an Offline notice says so: it shows what's already loaded, and changes need a connection. Keeping recent information on the phone comes next. See When the signal drops.

    🔔

    Notifications

    Important messages reach staff's phones even when BiG is closed, and each one waits on their Billboard. See Notifications, Announcements and My Profile › Notifications.

    🔄

    Always up to date

    New releases arrive by themselves. A short New version available message with a Reload button lets people switch when it suits them.

    When the signal drops Coming next

    Why Admit needs a connection

    Two gates without signal could each admit the same ticket. Once someone is inside the venue, that can't be undone. So Admit always checks the latest record first. People already signed in stay signed in while offline.

    • Booking details kept on phones: only on Box Office staff's phones, for finding bookings offline. They're removed when the person signs out, and three days after the event ends.
    • Hospitality: the Serving desk needs a connection today. Saving desk scans on the phone and sending them when back online is planned with working without signal.
    • Workshop door: planned to save scans on the phone and send them when back online. Today the lists and the door need a connection.

    Dry run: one happy path per area

    Before the festival, each team walks through its area once on a phone, so the first real gate, door or meal isn't the first try.

    Each part below says who does it, what must be ready first, the steps in order, and a few checks worth trying on purpose. The screenshots are phone views with invented sample people; your own pages show your own event's data.

    Report what you find

    If anything behaves unexpectedly, note the area, the step number and take a screenshot.

    Before anything: people and the event Directors, Admins, Developers

    Before you start

    • Sign in and check the sidebar footer shows the latest version. If it doesn't, tap Reload when BiG offers an update.
    • An active event exists in Admin › Events and is picked in the sidebar. Every page works on that event.
    • The Box Office, Workshop and Hospitality departments exist in Admin › Departments, with the right key (see department keys).

    Steps

    1. In Admin › Users, add staff one at a time with + New User, or many at once with Bulk Add (see Adding many people at once).
    2. Give each person their home department, food preference, gender and date of joining. Meal counts and beds use these.
    3. Put every Team Lead and Volunteer on the event's staff list: tick them on Users and tap Add to the event (see Who works each event). People created while the event is picked are added automatically.
    4. Ask everyone to sign in once and turn on Notifications in My Profile.
    Checks to try
    • Bulk Add: a weak password, a repeated email or an unknown department shows as a Problem or a Duplicate and is skipped. Only Ready rows are added.
    • Staff list: remove a Volunteer from the event. They no longer see it and are told they're not on its staff list.
    • Delete: someone with attendance history is deactivated, not deleted (see deleting several people).

    Box Office: admit a participant Box Office team, Directors, Admins

    Before you start

    • The event has its pass types, a Festival Pass and any tent passes, in Admin › Events.
    • Tents are created in Accommodation › Setup if you'll allocate tents.
    • Bookings are in, either imported with Bulk Upload on Bookings or made at the gate with Spot Registration.
    • You're on the event's staff list and have wrist bands and a phone with a camera.

    Steps

    1. On Registration, tap Scan QR for the participant's ticket email, or search by name, phone or email.
    2. On Booking Found, fill in any missing attendee details with the counter form.
    3. Tap Admit on the Festival Pass and scan a wrist band. The pass shows Admitted with its band.
    4. For a tent pass, tap Allocate Tent and pick a tent of that type.
    5. On Bookings, send or resend the Ticket Email if needed and check its status.
    Checks to try
    • Admit without a band isn't possible. A band already linked to someone else is refused, naming who has it.
    • Scanning the same booking again shows the pass as Admitted with its band. It doesn't admit it a second time.
    • A QR that isn't a booking shows "not found".
    • Lost band: Replace Band asks for a reason and keeps the history (see Wrist bands).

    Workshops: plan a session and run the door Workshop team

    The Workshop Team Lead plans the sessions. The whole team runs the lists and the door.

    Before you start

    • The venue boundary is drawn, and each venue's Workshop area is drawn on the Venue Map.
    • Participants are admitted with a wrist band at the Box Office. The band is how they're found at a workshop.

    Steps

    1. In Setup, add venues (with capacity and their map area) and workshops (title, category, facilitator).
    2. In the Planner, tap an empty slot (or drag on a desktop) to add a session. By default, registration opens 1 hour before the start, closes 30 minutes before, and the cut-off is 15 minutes before. Change these if needed.
    3. Open the session. On List, add participants in order by scanning their band or searching.
    4. At the start, switch to Door and scan bands. People on the list go in; others join as walk-ins or wait.
    5. Afterwards, check the numbers per session and per workshop in Reports.
    Checks to try
    • Over the list limit: you get a warning but can still add people. Extra people go on the waitlist and move up automatically.
    • Door timing: before registration closes, the door lets nobody in. Until the cut-off, only the list goes in; after it, everyone does.
    • Entry closed by hand blocks the door until it's opened again.
    • Scanning someone already inside shows "Already in".
    • Venue Map: the Workshop area shows the live status (see Workshops on the map).

    Hospitality: count, serve and report a meal Hospitality team

    Before you start

    • Staff are on the event's staff list and have their food preference and date of joining set. Counting starts on the mess opening day, and each person counts from their date of joining.
    • Team Leads mark attendance in My Department. Each meal counts one attendance slot; see Who's counted at meals.
    • Each staff member has their meal QR in My Profile, or a staff wrist band.

    Steps

    1. In Setup, set the mess dates, the meal times and, if you want, the Mess Hall area on the map.
    2. On Today, check the next meal's kitchen count (Veg / Non-veg) and who isn't marked yet. Use Remind TLs, then send the count to the kitchen.
    3. When the meal opens, use the Serving desk: scan the QR or search, then serve.
    4. For visitors, add a guest and give them a Meal Pass. They show its QR at the desk.
    5. After the meal, close the Mess Hall, add the kitchen figures (prepared, left over) and review the day in Report.
    6. If you want, give staff and guests beds in Stay.
    Checks to try
    • No attendance marked: the desk asks for a reason to serve, or lets you refuse. Notify TLs sends the flagged people to their Team Lead.
    • Already ate: blocked, unless you allow a second plate.
    • Not on the event's staff list: the desk shows "no meal".
    • Meal Pass: plates go down as they're served. An expired pass, or one for another meal, is refused.
    • Directors, Admins and Developers are always counted, without attendance.

    Venue Map: find and mark places Everyone views

    Team Leads add and edit their own department's pins and areas. Directors and Admins can edit anything and import KML files.

    Before you start

    • A Director or Admin has drawn the event's venue boundary. Team Leads can only draw inside it.

    Steps

    1. Open the map, switch between Street and Satellite, tap a pin or area, and use Directions or Street View.
    2. Add a pin: tap the map or use Use my location, then pick a type (water, first aid, toilets, stage, gate…) and your department.
    3. Pins start out locked. To move one, tap the lock button, drag the pin, then lock again (see the pin lock).
    4. Draw an area, such as a Workshop area or the Mess Hall, and give it its type.
    Checks to try
    • Scrolling the map while pins are locked moves no pins.
    • A Team Lead can't edit another department's pin or draw outside the boundary.
    • A Volunteer sees the map but has no edit or lock buttons.

    Public Page: the festival page for participants Directors, Admins, Developers

    Participants open the page at rchomeproject.in/live, without signing in. See Public Page for everything it does.

    Before you start

    • The event is active and has its start and end dates. Only events like this can be shown.
    • Workshop sessions are planned, so Live now, Up next and the Schedule have something to show.
    • Venue Map pins and areas are in place, for "Find your way" and the map.

    Steps

    1. In Admin › Public Page, switch the page On and pick the event to show (see Settings).
    2. Set how many days before and after the festival the page shows. Before the festival it shows a countdown; afterwards, a thank-you.
    3. Add up to 3 emergency contacts (the first is the main line), up to 4 help tiles, and the "through the years" numbers.
    4. Upload photos for the highlights. Switch SOS on and pick the departments that answer it (see the next part).
    5. Tap Open page and check it on a phone: Live now, Up next, Schedule, Map, News and the Emergency button (see What participants see and The Emergency button).
    6. On the public page, star a session to add it to My plan, and allow alerts to get reminders on that phone (see My plan and festival alerts).
    Checks to try
    • Page off, or outside the days set: the page says it's resting.
    • No staff or participant names or phone numbers appear anywhere. Facilitator names are the only exception.
    • An announcement sent to the public page appears in News and the ticker. Remove from public page takes it down at once (see Public page news).
    • Install: the public page can be added to a phone's home screen on its own.

    SOS: answer a participant's call for help Directors, Admins, Developers and the SOS departments

    Before you start

    • In Admin › Public Page, the page is on, SOS is on, the SOS departments are chosen and an emergency contact is set.
    • Responders are on the event's staff list and have notifications turned on in My Profile.

    Steps

    1. On a second phone, open rchomeproject.in/live and tap Emergency › Send SOS. Allow location and add a short message.
    2. Responders get an urgent notification and a note on their Billboard.
    3. Open the SOS page. See the location on the map, then tap Directions or Call.
    4. When it's dealt with, add a short note and mark it handled.
    Checks to try
    • SOS switched off: the button disappears from the public page.
    • Privacy: SOS locations and phone numbers are deleted automatically 30 days after the event.

    Billboard: read your notes Everyone

    1. Open Billboard from the menu. The red number shows how many notes are unread.
    2. Tap a note to open it, which marks it read. Mark all read clears the rest.
    3. Tapping a phone notification opens the matching note.
    Checks to try
    • Muted notifications: the note still appears on the Billboard.
    • Private notes, such as a Team Lead's attendance reminder, appear only for that person.

    Announcements: tell the team Directors, Admins, Developers

    1. In Admin › Announcements, pick a type (General, Urgent / Safety, Schedule change, Weather, Reminder or Task). Write a title of up to 60 characters and a message of up to 240.
    2. Choose the audience: roles, departments or both. Only the event's staff is on by default; untick it to reach everyone.
    3. If you want, also post it on the Public page for participants (see Public page news).
    4. Check the Goes to line and the preview, then tap Send and confirm.
    5. In the Sent list, use Send again or Copy to form.
    Checks to try
    • No audience picked: Send stays off.
    • Urgent / Safety reaches people even if they muted announcements.
    • Task and Reminder can't go on the public page.
    • Sent notes can't be recalled from phones. A public post can be removed from the public page.
