    Introduction

    BiG is a web platform that runs the operational side of a festival, from the first ticket sale to the last volunteer shift.

    Why this guide exists

    This guide explains what the platform does today, how each workflow runs from start to finish, where its current limits are, and what's coming next. It's written for organisers, directors, partners and anyone deciding how the festival should run, not for developers.

    How to read it

    Skim

    Read Platform at a glance and Platform strengths. It takes about five minutes.

    Explore

    Chapters 2–7 include hands-on demos. Click, tick and step through them. They use sample data and change nothing real.

    Plan

    Current limitations and the Roadmap show what's missing and what's next.

    About the demos

    Every interactive example runs entirely in this page with made-up names and numbers. Nothing you do here touches the real platform or its data.

    Conventions

    • Bold names are what you see on screen in the app, e.g. Bookings, Booking Found, Inventory.
    • Roles are written as short codes: DEV (Developer), DIR (Director), ADMIN, TL (Team Lead) and VOL (Volunteer).
    • Terms are defined in Appendix A — Glossary.

    How the pieces connect Interactive

    Everything in BiG belongs to one of two families. Festival records (events, passes, bookings, tents) start fresh with each edition.     People, roles and access

    Everyone sees exactly what their job needs. Access depends on three things: a person's role, the department they belong to, and any extra access they've been granted.

    See the platform through each role's eyes Interactive

    Full access Limited to their own area Only if granted / in the right department No access

    Access tiers: Read, Write, Manage Interactive

    Besides their home department, anyone can be given access to other departments, at one of three tiers. Each tier includes the ones below it.

    Read

    See the department's pages and information.

    Write

    Also make changes and carry out day-to-day actions.

    Manage

    Also oversee the department and the people in it.

    Team Leads manage automatically

    A Team Lead always has Manage access to their own department(s). Nobody has to remember to grant it, and it can't drift out of sync.

    Example: a volunteer helping out at Box Office

    A volunteer whose home department is Operations is needed at the gate for the weekend. An admin gives them Read access to Box Office. They can now open Bookings and Registration without moving departments, and their attendance stays with Operations. Removing the grant afterwards takes them back to normal.

    3

    Dashboard and sign-in

    Signing in takes people to a dashboard of the active event's headline numbers.

    Sign in

    Email and password. Repeated failed attempts pause sign-in for a while. Sessions last up to 7 days. If one expires mid-task, the next action returns you to sign-in with a notice, so nobody keeps working on a page that has silently stopped saving.

    Dashboard

    Passes sold, admitted, pending admission and accommodation bookings, plus volunteers by department for admins. Charts below show admitted vs. yet-to-admit across festival passes, tents and vehicles.

    Confirmations for deletes, vacates and overwrites appear as branded in-app dialogs. The sidebar footer always shows the current version.

    Try it: mark a day's attendance Demo

    VolunteerMorningAfternoonNight
    Why slots instead of whole days?

    Volunteers often move mid-day: Operations in the morning, Box Office in the afternoon. Recording each slot separately means both departments get credit for the right hours. One department can't silently overwrite another's record: the platform asks first.

    History and calendar

    The History tab summarises each member's attendance over a date range. The Calendar tab shows everyone against every date, with each cell marked, unmarked or not applicable (for example before someone's date of joining). It opens scrolled to today.

    Box Office

    Box Office handles a booking from the day it's imported to the moment the attendee walks through the gate. It has two halves: Bookings for the back office and Registration for the gate.

    The gate, step by step Simulator

    Pick a scenario and step through it. The diagram highlights where you are.

    Bookings: the back office

    Overview

    A searchable, paged list of every booking for the active event, with each booking's Ticket Email status. Headline numbers (Ticket Emails Sent, Admitted, Spot Registrations) double as one-tap filters, and a status filter finds bookings not sent, bounced or failed.

    Detail panel

    Selecting a booking slides in a panel with its summary, the number of attendees and passes, an Edit Ticket form, live attendee actions, email buttons and Email history, and Open in Registration.

    Bulk Upload

    Bring in bookings sold elsewhere from an Excel file. Drag it in, pick the sheet, and every row is checked before anything is saved.

    Export

    Download every loaded booking as an Excel file, one row per pass, in the same layout Bulk Upload accepts, so it can be re-imported.

    Try Bulk Upload's checks Demo

    Below is a sample file. Press Check file to see what the platform catches before saving, then fix the issue it allows you to fix on the spot.

    OrderItemQtyBuyerEmailResult
    What Bulk Upload checks
    • Required details on every row: order, item, quantity, buyer name, phone and email.
    • Duplicates: an order number that already exists for the event is rejected, so re-uploading a file never doubles bookings.
    • Unknown items: an item name that doesn't match the event's pass types is flagged. Staff can create it as a new pass type, with a category, right there.
    • Grouping: rows with the same order number become one booking with several passes.
    • Workbooks with several sheets: staff pick which sheet to import.

    Registration: the gate

    The Registration page opens with live numbers: Admitted, Pending Admits, Incomplete Details and Spot Registrations. Staff find a booking by scanning the QR code from its ticket email or by searching by name, phone, email or order number. Every scanned code is checked first: one that has been altered or made up is rejected with a message, and the scanner keeps running. Either way staff land on Booking Found, which lists every attendee on the booking and offers the same email buttons and history as the detail panel.

    Admit, not Check In

    At the gate the step is called Admit. "Check In" is kept for a planned online step, where each participant fills in their forms before arriving through a secure one-time link.

    Link Band In development

    Not complete: in progress

    Band Linking is still being built. Today Link Band is a demo: it scans the wrist band and shows what was read, but nothing is saved to the pass yet. Saving starts once the band ID format is decided.

    Link Band appears on Festival Pass rows on Booking Found and in the Bookings detail panel, next to Admit. It shows once the attendee's details are complete, both before and after admission, because a band may be handed out later.

    1. Tap Link Band on the attendee's Festival Pass. The scanner opens as Link Wrist Band, with the attendee's name.
    2. Scan the wrist band. The camera stops after the first read.
    3. Check what was read. Tap Scan again to retry, or Done to close.

    What comes next

    • Next: decide the band ID format, then save each band against its Festival Pass. Participants don't have profiles yet, so the band attaches to their ticket for now.
    • Later: once the Workshop pages exist, staff scan the band at each workshop venue to mark workshop attendance.

    Where band scanning is used

    • Today: Box Office, through Link Band (demo).
    • Planned: Workshops, for attendance at each session.
    How tent allocation keeps things right
    • Linked to a person: a tent pass must be tied to a festival pass holder. The platform suggests the likely match, even when they bought on a different booking.
    • Right tent type: only tents of the matching tent pass type are offered.
    • Never overfilled: two staff members can't both take the last place in a tent. The platform settles that safely.
    • Gender-aware: mixing genders in a tent needs a deliberate staff confirmation.

    Spot Registration

    For walk-ups with no booking, staff create one on the spot. They choose items from the active event's pass types, enter the buyer's details, and the platform assigns an order number automatically. With Email ticket to buyer ticked (the default, for staff who can send ticket emails), the buyer gets their ticket email straight away. The new booking goes straight to Booking Found and is admitted like any other. Spot registrations are counted separately, so walk-up demand is visible at a glance.

    Accommodation

    Accommodation is where the physical tents are built and kept accurate. Putting people into tents happens at the gate in Box Office. Both work on the same live tent records.

    Inventory Box Office team & admins

    • Search by unit number or occupant name. Filter by tent type and by occupancy. Numbers sort naturally (SO2 before SO10).
    • Rows are colour-coded by how full they are, and expand to show each occupant's details and admission status.
    • Edit a unit's number or capacity. Capacity can't drop below the number of people already in it.
    • Delete a unit only when it's empty.
    • Vacate an occupant directly, after a confirmation.

    Try it: allocate and vacate Demo

    Pick an attendee, then click Allocate on a tent. Click an occupied place to vacate it. Watch the occupancy cards update.

    Setup Admins

    1. Pick a tent pass type of the active event. If it has no short code yet, you're asked for one once (e.g. SO for Solo Tent).
    2. Enter a capacity and a quantity. The platform creates that many numbered units (SO1, SO2, …), continuing from any that already exist.
    3. Check the occupancy cards for each tent type: units open, places vacant, wholly vacant and full tents, split by Male, Female and Mixed.
    7

    Admin

    The Admin pages set up the organisation: its people, its departments, each festival edition, and a record of every email sent. Each page is open only to the roles shown next to it.

    Users Admins; Team Leads for their own department

    People and departments are set up once for the whole organisation and carry across every event.

    Finding people

    Admin › Users follows the same layout as Bookings. Summary cards at the top (Admins, Team Leads, Volunteers and No Department) count each group and filter the table when tapped. Below them, staff can search by name or email, filter by role and department, and page through a table sorted by name. Selecting a person opens a side panel with their department, access level, dates and access in each department, plus the actions they're allowed to take.

    Adding a person

    1. An admin taps + New User, which opens the same side panel ready for a new account, and fills in name, email, role and Date of Joining.
    2. They choose the person's home department.
    3. They tap access chips to grant Read, Write or Manage in any other department.
    4. The person signs in with a password that must mix upper- and lower-case letters, a number and a symbol. The form checks that the password is typed the same way twice.
    Team Leads look after their own people

    A TL can open the Users page and adjust access for people in their own department. They can't create or remove accounts, change anyone's role or move people between departments, and grants to other departments are left untouched.

    Removing a person Admins

    Before anything happens, the Delete confirmation says which of two outcomes applies:

    • Deleted: someone with no records, such as an account created by mistake, is removed for good.
    • Deactivated: someone with attendance records, or who marked attendance or created events, is switched off instead, so the festival's history stays complete. They can't sign in, and any open session ends straight away. They disappear from user lists, department pages and attendance marking, while their past attendance still shows under their name.

    Directors and Developers have an extra Deactivated card and status filter, and can Reactivate an account with the same role, department and access as before. Nobody can remove their own account, and admins can't remove Directors or Developers.

    Departments Admins

    Create & describe

    Each department has a name and a description, and its card shows the Team Lead(s) and volunteer count.

    Assign people

    Add Team Leads and volunteers by typing their names. A volunteer belongs to one department, so moving one shows a warning first. Leads and admins can belong to several.

    Remove people

    Taking someone out of a department also removes the access that came with it.

    Delete safely

    Deleting a department that still has people shows who's affected. Confirming unassigns everyone, then deletes it.

    Events and pass types Admins

    Each festival edition is its own event, with its own passes, bookings and tents. Staff choose which event they're working on from the selector in the sidebar.

    Pass types

    Admins define the passes sold for each event. Every pass type belongs to one of three categories, and the category decides how the rest of the platform treats it.

    Festival

    Admission to the festival. Each festival pass is one person, which is how the platform counts attendees and admissions.

    Tent

    A place to stay. Tent pass types get a short code (e.g. SO) and their own numbered tent units in Accommodation.

    Add-on

    Anything sold alongside a pass. It's tracked on the booking but doesn't need a person or a tent.

    Setting up a new edition

    1. Create the event in Admin › Events. It starts as a Draft, visible to admins only.
    2. Add its pass types and give each a category.
    3. Check the ticket email wording. Under Ticket email, set the Subject and Intro paragraph buyers will see, or leave them empty to use a default built from the event's name.
    4. Build the tents for each tent pass type in Accommodation › Setup.
    5. Set it to Active. Team Leads and volunteers can now select it and start work.
    6. After the festival, archive it. Everything is kept, and it disappears from day-to-day lists.
    Real bookings are protected

    An event or pass type that already has bookings or tents can't be deleted. Archiving is the safe way to retire it.

    Email Report Admins

    Admin › Email Report lists every email the platform has sent, in the same layout as Bookings. Summary cards show Sent, Delivered, Bounced, Failed and Opened (approx.). Staff can search, and filter by type, send status, delivery, event, sender and date range. Selecting an email shows its full details, how its delivery progressed and, for custom emails, the message as written. Export downloads the filtered list as an Excel file.

    8

    My Profile

    My Profile shows each person exactly what they can access, and is where they manage their own photo. It opens from their name at the bottom of the sidebar.

    It shows your photo, which you can add, change or remove. It also shows your role, departments, a colour-coded table of your access in each department, and your own attendance since your date of joining.

    Profile photos

    People can add a photo from My Profile, either by uploading one from their device or by taking one with the camera, then dragging and zooming to frame a round crop. Admins can set or remove anyone's photo from the Users panel. The photo appears in the Users page, the sidebar, My Profile, Departments and My Department. People without a photo show their initial. Photos are saved as small thumbnails, so pages stay fast on festival-site connections.

    Platform strengths

    These qualities make the platform dependable on festival day.

    🛡

    Built to protect real data

    Bookings, events and occupied tents can't be deleted by accident. Removing a person who has records deactivates them instead of erasing their history. Overwrites ask first, and destructive actions need confirmation.

    ⚖

    Safe under pressure

    When several staff work the same booking or tent at once, the platform settles it cleanly. No double allocations, no overfilled tents.

    🎯

    Right access for every person

    Five roles, three access tiers and department membership combine, so a volunteer sees only their work and a director sees everything.

    📱

    Works on any screen

    Every page is designed for phones as well as desktops, so gate staff can work from a handheld device.

    🔁

    Ready for every edition

    Events and pass types are configured, not hard-coded. A new festival year needs setup, not development.

    ✅

    Tested and staged releases

    Hundreds of automated checks run before every release. Each release is tried in a test environment before wider use.

    🔗

    Fits existing sales channels

    Bookings sold elsewhere come in through Bulk Upload. Export gives a re-importable backup at any time.

    🧭

    Honest records

    Attendance by slot, admissions per person, tents linked to the right festival pass, and a record of every email sent and whether it arrived. The numbers can be trusted.

    Coming next: BiG as an installable app

    BiG will become an app that staff keep on their phone's home screen: quicker to open, steadier on weak signal, and able to send notifications.

    In development

    Work has started. The first steps, preparing sign-in for the installed app and then the home-screen app itself, are underway. This chapter describes the plan agreed with the festival team; details may change as it's built and tested.

    What changes for staff

    📲

    On your home screen

    Add BiG from the browser in a few taps. No app store, no download, no new account. It works on Android phones, iPhones and computers.

    🖥

    Opens like an app

    BiG gets its own icon and opens full screen, without the address bar and tabs. That leaves more room for the gate and attendance screens.

    ⚡

    Quick to start

    The app itself is kept on the phone, so it opens quickly even when the signal is weak.

    📶

    Copes with patchy signal

    No more endless spinners. BiG shows when the connection is slow or gone, and the most useful screens keep working. See When the signal drops.

    🔔

    Notifications

    Important messages reach staff's phones even when BiG is closed. See Notifications.

    🔄

    Always up to date

    New releases arrive by themselves. A short New version available message lets people reload when it suits them.

    When the signal drops

    Festival grounds, and sometimes even Box Office Wi-Fi, lose signal. Every part of BiG follows one of four clear rules, so staff always know what will work.

    Without signal…Applies to

    👁 Shows the last saved copy, labelled with its time (e.g. "As of 10:42")Dashboard · My Profile · My Department roster and history · Bookings list · finding a booking at the gate
    💾 Saved on the phone, sent automatically when back onlineMarking volunteer attendance

    Why Admit needs a connection

    Two gates without signal could each admit the same ticket. Once someone is inside the venue, that can't be undone. So Admit always checks the latest record first. People already signed in stay signed in while offline.

    • Booking details kept on phones: only on Box Office staff's phones, for finding bookings offline. They're removed when the person signs out, and three days after the event ends.
    • Hospitality: the planned mess-hall scanning will be designed to work without signal from the start.

    Why the installable app

    Staff use a mix of Android phones and iPhones, and admin work happens on laptops. One app covers all of them, with no store fees or review delays, so improvements reach everyone the same day. If a hardware feature ever becomes essential, the same app can later be packaged for the stores without starting over.

    How it will arrive

    1. The installable app: home-screen icon, full-screen view, quick start, a clear offline screen and the New version available message.
    2. Notifications: announcements, "email sending finished", and notification settings in My Profile.
    3. Working without signal: the rules in When the signal drops.

    Later: meal calls and attendance reminders arrive with the Hospitality page. If outages turn out to be frequent, one chosen backup gate phone could keep admitting people during an outage, with safeguards against double entry. That will be decided after the first festival with the app.
