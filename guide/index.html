    Introduction

    BiG is a web platform that runs the operational side of a festival, from the first ticket sale to the last volunteer shift.

    Why this guide exists

    This guide explains what the platform does today, how each workflow runs from start to finish, where its current limits are, and what's coming next. It's written for organisers, directors, partners and anyone deciding how the festival should run, not for developers.

    How the pieces connect Interactive

    Everything in BiG belongs to one of two families. Festival records (events, passes, bookings, tents, patient reports, WASH facilities and volunteer deposits) start fresh with each edition. People records (users, departments, attendance, and the tasks sent to them) are shared across every event, so a volunteer's history carries over from one year to the next. Each event keeps a staff list of the people who work it. Hospitality's mess plan, guests, meal passes and beds belong to an event too, while the staff they feed and house are people records. Pick a flow to follow it, or click any box.

    Each event's staff list

    Roles, departments and access are the same for every event. What changes from one event to the next is its staff list: who works that event. Team Leads and volunteers can open only the active events they're on, and only the people on it are counted for that event's meals, beds, attendance, announcements and SOS alerts. Developers, Directors and Admins are always on every event. See Who works each event.

    Dashboard, Billboard, SOS and sign-in

    Signing in takes people to a dashboard of the active event, the Billboard keeps every announcement and task sent to them, and the SOS page brings participants' calls for help to the right staff.

    Sign in

    Email and password. Capital letters in the email don't matter, and the eye button shows or hides the password as you type. Repeated failed attempts pause sign-in for a while. Sessions last up to 7 days. If one expires mid-task, the next action returns you to sign-in with a notice, so nobody keeps working on a page that has silently stopped saving.

    Dashboard

    Developers, Directors, Admins and the Helpdesk get a festival overview of the event picked in the sidebar. It has a Needs attention now strip, headline tiles and a section each for the Gate, Accommodation, People, Hospitality, Workshops, WASH, the Medical Desk (numbers only) and Comms & safety. Every panel links to its page, revenue shows for Directors only, and it refreshes every minute. Team Leads and volunteers get a simple view: their team's (or own) attendance today, the next meal, their latest Billboard notes and their bed tonight.

    Confirmations for deletes, vacates and overwrites appear as branded in-app dialogs. In every side panel and dialog, Close or Cancel sits on the left and the main action on the right, on phones too. The top of the sidebar shows the name and dates of the event you're working on, and the sidebar footer always shows the current version.

    • Messages: a success message closes by itself after 4 seconds (a thin bar shows the time left, and pointing at it pauses it). An error stays until you close it, so it can't be missed.
    • Forms say what's wrong: a field with a problem gets a red outline and a short message underneath, and Save lists every problem instead of doing nothing. The Users and Volunteers forms work this way first; other forms follow.

    The sidebar has Main (Dashboard, Billboard, SOS, Venue Map and My Department) and one item per section: Box Office, Accommodation, Workshops, Music, Hospitality, Medical Desk, WASH, Volunteers and Admin. A section's pages are tabs in the page header, under the page title. Clicking a section opens the tab you used last there, in that browser.

    • Every tab has its own address, so refreshing, the browser's Back button, bookmarks and notification links stay on the same page.
    • A thin Back strip on the left of the tab row goes up from a page opened from a tab, such as a booking, a workshop session or an event, and otherwise to the previous page.
    • People see only the sections and tabs they may open. With only one tab left, the tab row is hidden.
    • On phones, long tab rows scroll sideways, and the tab row hides while scrolling down and comes back on scroll up.
    • Details on many pages slide in from the right on a desktop and up from the bottom on phones. Close them with ✕, Esc or a tap outside.

    Billboard Everyone

    Billboard, under Main in the sidebar, keeps every announcement sent to you, pinned up as post-it notes with the newest first. It's the place to catch up on anything you missed, even if you never turned notifications on or have muted them.

    • One colour per type. Each note takes its type's colour (General, Urgent, Schedule, Weather, Reminder, Task, Meals or SOS) and shows the title, the message and when it was sent.
    • A NEW sticker marks a note you haven't opened yet.
    • Tap a note to open it as a large note with the full message, From (who sent it), To (who it went to) and Sent (date and time).
    • The All and Unread tabs switch between every note and only the new ones. Mark all read clears every NEW sticker at once.
    • The newest 100 notes show first. Show older brings in earlier ones.
    • A red number next to Billboard in the sidebar shows how many notes are unread.

    The board fills the page width on a desktop. On phones, notes sit two to a row.

    Where notes come from

    • Announcements sent to you from Admin › Announcements.
    • Emails sent: when a ticket or custom email send you started has finished, a private note from BiG, addressed to "Only you", gives how many were sent and how many failed.
    • Meal alerts from the Hospitality team, to the event's staff.
    • Hospitality tasks for Team Leads: a private Task addressed to "Only you". It either asks you to mark attendance before a meal's kitchen count is due, or lists people from your team who ate without attendance marked. Tasks sent automatically show "From: BiG".
    • SOS alerts: for the people who handle SOS, a private red note for each help request from the public festival page. See SOS.
    • Tasks from Announcements show their status. Opening one shows the status, the buttons that move it on, and its timeline. Whoever sent a task also has it on their own Billboard, and it turns unread again when the task changes. See Tasks.
    • WASH alerts: for the WASH team on the event and DEV / DIR / ADMIN, a note when a facility goes overdue, is marked Empty or Out of order, or stock runs low. See WASH.
    • Tapping a BiG notification on your phone or computer opens the Billboard with that note already open.

    Announcements sent before the Billboard was added aren't on it.

    SOS Admins and chosen departments

    Participants can ask the festival team for help from the Emergency button on the public festival page. Each request arrives on the SOS page, under Main in the sidebar, which shows a red count of open requests.

    • Who handles it: Directors, Admins, Developers and members of the departments picked on Admin › Public Page, such as a medical or security team. Only they see SOS in the sidebar.
    • The alert: each request arrives as a notification and as a private red note on their Billboard. It comes through even when notifications are muted. Department members get it only if they're on the staff list of the event shown on the public page.
    • Each request shows when it was sent, a small map, how accurate the location is, Directions, the participant's message and call-back number (tap to call), and Outside the venue when it came from beyond the festival ground.
    • Mark handled, with an optional note, records who handled it and when. Open requests stay at the top.
    • The page refreshes itself every 15 seconds. Tapping the notification opens that request.
    • Locations and phone numbers are deleted automatically 30 days after the event.
    • For Medical Desk users, Start medical visit opens the Medical Desk with the SOS message and phone filled in.

    SOS is off until it's switched on in Admin › Public Page. Anyone on the public page can send one, so the page reminds staff to treat it like a phone call and check before acting.

    Venue Map

    The Venue Map shows the festival ground for the active event: the places that matter, the zones and routes between them, and what's happening there right now, from workshops and meals to water stations, the Medical camp, camping fields and open tasks.

    Venue Map, under Main in the sidebar, is open to everyone signed in. It shows a Street map or a Satellite view. Each event has its own map, so a new venue starts clean. On a desktop the map stays in place and the side panel scrolls. On phones the lists sit below the map, and picking an item scrolls back up to it.

    Pointing at a pin, an area or its badge shows a short tooltip. On phones, tapping it shows the same information in a small popup.

    Locations

    A location is a single place on the ground, shown as a coloured pin. Each has a name, a type (Water station, Toilets, Stage, Gate, Entry point, Box Office, Tent field, Helpdesk or Other), an optional responsible department and notes. Each event has at most one Box Office pin. Helpdesk pins also show on the public map, so participants can find help.

    • Add location, then tap the map, or tap Use my location to drop a pin where you're standing. BiG shows how accurate the phone's position is.
    • Pins are locked every time the map opens, so scrolling on a phone can't move one by accident. People who can edit pins see a lock button next to Street / Satellite. Tap it to unlock, then drag a pin to fine-tune it. A pin open in its form can always be moved. Edit or delete a pin from its card.
    • Type filters with counts, and a list beside the map, help find a place quickly.
    • Directions and Street View open Google Maps at that spot. Go to jumps to pasted coordinates.
    • Each location shows which zone it's in, with a warning if it's outside the festival boundary.

    Areas and lines

    Areas mark zones on the ground and lines mark routes and edges. Each has a name, a kind, a responsible department and notes, and shows its kind's colour and icon on the map.

    My Department and volunteer attendance

    Every role has a My Department page, scoped to what they should see. Team Leads mark attendance by slot (Morning, Afternoon, Night), so the record shows who worked, where and when.

    RoleWhat they see
    DEV / DIR / ADMINAny department, plus an All view across departments with Daily Summary, Department Calendar and All Users tabs. Tapping a department there opens it.
    TLTheir own department(s): marks attendance for their team, opens each member's details and changes a member's role.
    VOLTheir own department, read-only. Their personal history also appears on My Profile.

    My Department follows the event picked in the sidebar. Its lists, history, calendar and the All view show only the people on that event's staff list, and the header says so. Attendance can be marked only for them, and attendance already recorded never changes. Switching the event in the sidebar reloads the page.

    The department sits in a header card with its icon, description, Team Leads, volunteers, volunteers needed and a Tasks marker. Below it, stat cards for the day show the people, how many are marked, Not marked (tap it to show only them) and open tasks, followed by the tabs. The All departments view has the same stat cards and tabs.

    • From the day they report: Team Leads and volunteers appear on the daily list from the day they're marked Reported (see Joining time). Someone not reported yet isn't listed and can't be marked.
    • Reporting marks the rest of that day: Morning if they reported before Lunch, Afternoon if before Dinner, and Night. Slots already marked are left alone, and later days are marked by the Team Lead as usual.
    • Volunteers needed: a department's Team Lead sets how many volunteers it needs for the event. See Volunteers.
    • Tasks: the header card reads, for example, "Tasks · 2 open" with a red dot, or "No tasks". Tapping it opens the department's tasks in a side panel, with the same status chips as on Announcements. See Tasks.

    Marking attendance

    Each person has a Morning, Afternoon and Night chip, filled when marked, and a count such as 2/3. The date has ‹ › steppers, a calendar on tap and Back to today. Team Leads get Mark all for each slot and a save bar that counts unsaved changes. Pointing at a slot shows who marked it and when, or "ticked, not saved yet". An automatic mark says it was made when the person reported.

    A member's details

    The department's Team Leads, and Directors, Admins and Developers, tap a person in Attendance, History or Calendar to open their details in a side panel. It's the same profile as on the Volunteers page: contact, details, joining time, staff band with Link band / Replace band, seven days of attendance and location on request.

    • Role: turns a volunteer into a Team Lead of the department, or back, after a confirmation. Nobody changes their own role, and Directors, Admins and Developers are never changed here. A Team Lead who leads more than one department is changed in Admin › Users.
    • Location on request: a Team Lead can ask their own department's volunteers where they are, and sees only those answers. The notification says their Team Lead asked.

    Why slots instead of whole days?

    Volunteers often move mid-day: Operations in the morning, Box Office in the afternoon. Recording each slot separately means both departments get credit for the right hours. One department can't silently overwrite another's record: the platform asks first.

    Attendance feeds the kitchen

    From the day after they report, Hospitality counts Team Leads and volunteers at a meal only when they're marked present in the slot that meal counts. Marking attendance on time keeps the kitchen's numbers right. See Who's counted at meals.

    History and calendar

    The History tab summarises each member's attendance over a date range. The Calendar tab shows everyone against every date, with each cell marked, unmarked or not applicable: before the day someone reported, or after their last mark in a department they've since left. People who haven't reported yet aren't on it. It opens scrolled to today.

    Bookings: the back office

    Overview

    A searchable, paged list of every booking for the active event. Each row shows the buyer, Booking ID, passes, amount, Payment method (Online, Offline or Guest list), Paid at, Source (Sales system, Spot registration or Bulk Upload), Ticket Email status, Festival details, Beds and Early Check-In status. Headline tiles (Ticket Emails Sent, Admitted, Spot Registrations, Details missing, Beds don't match) double as one-tap filters.

    Search and filters

    Search by name, email, phone, Booking ID, ticket, payment reference, payment method, source or the date paid (for example "10 nov"). Filters for Ticket Email status, Early Check-In status and pass type combine with the search and the tiles. Tap a column header to sort.

    Detail panel

    Selecting a booking slides in a panel with its Booking ID and payment references, the total, Ticket Email, Source, Festival details, Beds and Early Check-In boxes, its linked bookings, an Edit Ticket form, every attendee action, email buttons, Email history and Open in Registration.

    Export

    Download every loaded booking as an Excel file, one row per pass, in the same layout Bulk Upload accepts, so it can be re-imported.

    Pass type filter and Selected only

    • The pass type dropdown lists every pass type of the event under Festival Passes, Stay Passes and Add-ons, each with how many bookings it shows. Tick several at once. A booking shows when it, or a booking linked to it, holds any ticked type.
    • Selected only, next to the selection count, shows just the bookings you've ticked. It's greyed out until you tick one, and switches itself off when the last tick is removed.

    Bookings from the online sales system

    One event at a time takes the online sales system's bookings: the one with Sales system switched on in Admin › Events. The sales system tells BiG straight away when a booking is made, changed or cancelled.

    • New bookings appear on Bookings with their Booking ID, payment method, time paid and payment references. One pass is made per person, bed or add-on bought.
    • Changed bookings are updated in place. An upgraded pass (for example Day Pass to Weekend Pass) keeps the person's details, admission, wrist band and bed. Anything already in use that a change can't touch safely is kept and listed on the booking under To check.
    • Cancelled bookings get a red Cancelled badge. Passes not in use are switched off; passes already in use are kept and flagged. A cancelled booking gets no Ticket Email or Early Check-In email.
    • Waiting to be stored: a booking that can't be stored yet, usually because the event doesn't have its pass type, waits with its reason and is tried again on the next pull. Directors, Admins and Developers can create the missing pass type right there, filled in from the sales system. Others see the list and are asked to contact an admin.
    • Pull from sales system opens a panel with the link's status, Pull now and a report: new, changed, cancelled, up to date, problems and pass types missing.
    • Check & repair, in the same panel: Check all bookings compares every paid booking in the sales system with BiG, stores any missing ones and lists bookings BiG holds that the sales system doesn't show as paid. Fetch one booking brings in a single booking by its Booking ID.

    How tent allocation keeps things right
    • Beds on a Stay Pass: a Stay Pass holds as many people as its pass type's Beds per pass, one bed each. Its row shows, for example, Beds 2 / 3, its unit and the people in its beds. Allocate bed or Allocate next bed shows while a bed is free; a one-bed pass keeps Allocate Tent.

    • Same unit by default: Allocate shows Bed 2 of 3 and who is already in, and picks the same unit as the other beds. Choosing another unit asks first, with Cancel / Allocate anyway.
    • Vacate one bed: with one bed taken, Vacate frees it. With several, each person has their own × to free only their bed.

    1. Create the event in Admin › Events. It starts as a Draft, visible to admins only.
    2. Add its pass types and give each a category. Give Stay Pass types their beds per pass, and give each the code the online sales system uses for it.
    3. Check the ticket email wording. Under Ticket email, set the Subject and Intro paragraph buyers will see, or leave them empty to use a default built from the event's name. Under Early Check-In email, do the same for the Early Check-In email.
    4. Switch on Sales system on the event's details if this event takes the online sales system's bookings. Only one event can have it on; switching it on asks first and names the event that loses it.
    5. Build the tents for each Stay Pass type in Accommodation › Setup, until Setup says there are enough beds.
    6. Draw the venue boundary. The event's page shows whether it's drawn, with a button that opens the Venue Map.
    7. Plan the workshops in Workshops › Setup and the Planner, if the edition has any.
    8. Plan the music: draw Stage areas on the Venue Map, then add stages, acts and slots in Music.
    9. Set up Hospitality: the mess calendar and meal times in Hospitality › Setup, the Mess Hall area on the Venue Map, and the staff and guest units in Hospitality › Stay.
    10. Fill the staff list. With the event picked in the sidebar, tick its Team Leads and volunteers on the Users page and tap Add to the event. The event's page shows how many people work it, with Manage on Users page.
    11. Set it to Active. Team Leads and volunteers on its staff list can now select it and start work. Setting an active event back to Draft or Archived asks first.
    12. Switch on the public page in Admin › Public Page, if participants should see it.
    13. After the festival, archive it. Everything is kept, and it disappears from day-to-day lists.

    Dry run: one happy path per area

    Before the festival, each team walks through its area once on a phone, so the first real gate, door or meal isn't the first try.

    Each part below says who does it, what must be ready first, the steps in order, and a few checks worth trying on purpose. The screenshots use invented sample people; your own pages show your own event's data.

    Report what you find

    If anything behaves unexpectedly, note the area, the step number and take a screenshot.

    Before anything: people and the event Directors, Admins, Developers

    Before you start

    • Sign in and check the sidebar footer shows the latest version. If it doesn't, tap Reload when BiG offers an update.
    • An active event exists in Admin › Events and is picked in the sidebar. Every page works on that event.
    • The departments for each area exist in Admin › Departments with the right key: Box Office, Accommodation, Workshops, Hospitality, Medical Desk, WASH, Volunteers and Helpdesk (see the access keys).

    Steps

    1. In Admin › Users, add staff one at a time with + New User, or many at once with Bulk Add (see Adding many people at once).
    2. Give each person their home department, food preference, gender and Expected Join Time. Meal counts and beds use these. When people arrive, mark them Reported (see Joining time).
    3. Put every Team Lead and Volunteer on the event's staff list: tick them on Users and tap Add to the event (see Who works each event). People created while the event is picked are added automatically.
    4. Ask everyone to sign in once and turn on Notifications in My Profile.
    Checks to try
    • Bulk Add: a weak password, a repeated email or an unknown department shows as a Problem or a Duplicate and is skipped. Only Ready rows are added.
    • Staff list: remove a Volunteer from the event. They no longer see it and are told they're not on its staff list.
    • Delete: someone with attendance history is deactivated, not deleted (see deleting several people).

    Box Office: admit a participant Box Office team, Directors, Admins

    Before you start

    • The event has its pass types, a Festival Pass and any Stay Passes with their beds per pass, in Admin › Events.
    • Tents are created in Accommodation › Setup if you'll allocate tents.
    • Bookings are in: from the online sales system, from Import on Bookings, or made at the gate with Spot Registration. Check the Details missing and Beds don't match tiles first (see Checking bookings).
    • You're on the event's staff list and have wrist bands and a phone with a camera.

    Steps

    1. On Registration, tap Scan QR for the participant's ticket email, or search by name, phone or email.
    2. On Booking Found, fill in any missing attendee details with the counter form.
    3. Tap Admit on the Festival Pass and scan a wrist band. The pass shows Admitted with its band.
    4. For a Stay Pass, tap Allocate Tent (Allocate bed for a pass with several beds) and pick a tent of that type. Fill each bed the same way; the next bed goes into the same unit unless you choose another.
    5. On Bookings, send or resend the Ticket Email if needed and check its status.
    Checks to try
    • Admit without a band isn't possible. A band already linked to someone else is refused, naming who has it.
    • Scanning the same booking again shows the pass as Admitted with its band. It doesn't admit it a second time.
    • A QR that isn't a booking shows "not found".
    • Lost band: Replace Band asks for a reason and keeps the history (see Wrist bands).

    Early Check-In: get everyone's details before the festival Box Office Team Lead, Directors, Admins, Developers

    Before you start

    • Bookings are in, and the buyers have real email addresses.
    • The Early Check-In email wording is set, or left empty for the default, in Admin › Events.

    Steps

    1. On Bookings, tap the Details missing tile and tick the bookings.
    2. Choose Email ▾ › Send Early Check-In, keep Send only to the bookings with details missing and 14 days, and tap Send test to me first.
    3. Open the test email, then send for real. The Early Check-In column shows Sent.
    4. As a buyer, open the link, fill in each Festival Pass and tap Check and send.
    5. Back on Bookings, the booking shows Submitted and its Festival details are complete.
    Checks to try
    • Open the link again after sending: it says the form was already sent.
    • Send a new link to the same booking: the old link stops working.
    • An admitted pass shows a lock on the form and can't be changed.
    • A wrong phone or email is pointed out under the field before the form can be sent.

    Workshops: plan a session and run the door Workshop team

    The Workshop Team Lead plans the sessions. The whole team runs the lists and the door.

    Before you start

    • The venue boundary is drawn, and each venue's Workshop area is drawn on the Venue Map.
    • Participants are admitted with a wrist band at the Box Office. The band is how they're found at a workshop.

    Steps

    1. In Setup, add venues (with capacity and their map area) and workshops (title, category, facilitator).
    2. In the Planner, tap an empty slot (or drag on a desktop) to add a session. By default, registration opens 1 hour before the start, closes 30 minutes before, and the cut-off is 15 minutes before. Change these if needed.
    3. Open the session. On List, add participants in order by scanning their band or searching.
    4. At the start, switch to Door and scan bands. People on the list go in; others join as walk-ins or wait.
    5. Afterwards, check the numbers per session and per workshop in Reports.
    Checks to try
    • Over the list limit: you get a warning but can still add people. Extra people go on the waitlist and move up automatically.
    • Door timing: before registration closes, the door lets nobody in. Until the cut-off, only the list goes in; after it, everyone does.
    • Entry closed by hand blocks the door until it's opened again.
    • Scanning someone already inside shows "Already in".
    • Venue Map: the Workshop area shows the live status (see Workshops on the map).

    Music: plan a stage's day Music team, Directors, Admins

    Before you start

    • A department with the Music key exists, with its Team Lead.
    • A Stage area is drawn on the Venue Map for each stage.

    Steps

    1. In Music › Setup, tap Add stage, pick its Stage area and tick its amenities.
    2. Tap Add act for two acts, with genre and contact.
    3. In the Planner, add a slot for each act on the stage, one with a sound check.
    4. Drag the second slot so it overlaps the first.
    5. Open the Venue Map and the public page and look at the stage.
    Checks to try
    • Overlapping slots: both show a Clash chip, and the slot can still be saved.
    • Public page: shows the artist, genre, stage and times only, never the contact, notes or sound check.
    • A stage with slots can't be deleted, and its Stage area can't be deleted on the Venue Map.
    • A Music member without Write access only views and taps a slot for its details.

    Hospitality: count, serve and report a meal Hospitality team

    Before you start

    • Staff are on the event's staff list and have their food preference and Expected Join Time set. Counting starts on the mess opening day. Each person counts from their Expected Join Time until they're marked Reported, and from the next day Team Leads and volunteers count by attendance.
    • Team Leads mark attendance in My Department. Each meal counts one attendance slot; see Who's counted at meals.
    • Each staff member has their meal QR in My Profile, or a staff wrist band.

    Steps

    1. In Setup, set the mess dates, the meal times and, if you want, the Mess Hall area on the map.
    2. On Today, check the next meal's kitchen count (Veg / Non-veg) and who isn't marked yet. Use Remind TLs, then send the count to the kitchen.
    3. When the meal opens, use the Serving desk: scan the QR, or tap the person in the Expected list and press Serve.
    4. For visitors, add a guest and give them a Meal Pass. They show its QR at the desk.
    5. After the meal, close the Mess Hall, add the kitchen figures (prepared, left over) and review the day in Report.
    6. If you want, give staff and guests beds in Stay.
    Checks to try
    • No attendance marked: the desk asks for a reason to serve, or lets you refuse. Notify TLs sends the flagged people to their Team Lead.
    • Already ate: blocked, unless you allow a second plate.
    • Not on the event's staff list: the desk shows "no meal".
    • Meal Pass: plates go down as they're served. An expired pass, or one for another meal, is refused.
    • Directors, Admins and Developers are counted without attendance once they've reported; before that, from their Expected Join Time.
    • Save count: change the Veg or Non-veg number before saving; the dialog says it differs from the expected count.
    • A tapped name never serves by itself: nothing is recorded until Serve.

    Medical Desk: record a visit Medical team, Directors, Developers

    Use made-up patients only. Health details from a dry run must never be real ones.

    Before you start

    • A department with the Medical Desk key exists, and the medical team are its members or have Medical Desk access.
    • They're on the event's staff list. A test participant has been admitted with a wrist band.

    Steps

    1. On Desk, scan the test participant's wrist band, or search for them.
    2. Start a visit: the complaint, vitals, severity, medicines given, the outcome and a follow-up.
    3. Open the patient report and check the visit on the timeline. Mark the follow-up done.
    4. Add a walk-in by hand, and record a visit with the outcome resting in the Medical camp. Later, tap Left the camp.
    5. Check the day on Report, then find both patients on Records.
    Checks to try
    • An Admin doesn't see the Medical Desk. The Dashboard shows them numbers only.
    • Change a visit recorded by someone else: only the Medical Team Lead, Directors and Developers can. Only they can delete one.
    • Lock hides the pages at once, and the password is asked again.
    • Email report to a test address: Admin › Email Report lists it only as Medical report.
    • Venue Map: the Medical camp shows how many are resting, never names (see the Medical camp on the map).

    WASH: keep a water station and a toilet block going WASH team

    Before you start

    • A department with the WASH key exists, and its team is on the event's staff list with notifications on.
    • Water or Toilets pins, or Washroom areas, are on the Venue Map. Setup can also drop a new pin.

    Steps

    1. In Setup, pick a water station and a toilet block. Set how often each is refilled or cleaned, and the water station's capacity.
    2. Print their QR stickers and put one on each.
    3. On Monitor, scan a sticker and log Refilled or Cleaned. The card turns OK, and the history says "via QR".
    4. Mark the water station Empty, and the toilet block Out of order with a note. Then tap Back in service.
    5. In Stock, add an item with a "Low at" number. Record Received, then Used.
    6. Check the day on Report, and tap Export.
    Checks to try
    • Require a QR scan on: Refilled and Cleaned ask for the sticker first. Empty and Out of order still work from anywhere.
    • Overdue, Empty or Out of order: the WASH team and Directors, Admins and Developers get an alert and a Billboard note.
    • Using more than the store holds warns, and the item goes to 0.
    • Deleting a WASH pin on the Venue Map is refused until it's removed in Setup.
    • The public page map shows only Cleaned, Open or Closed and Filled or Empty, never who or when.

    Volunteers: place volunteers where they're needed Volunteer Team Lead, Directors, Admins

    Before you start

    • A department with the Volunteers key exists, with its Team Lead. It's the Unassigned pool.
    • Volunteers are on the event's staff list. Some departments have set Volunteers needed in My Department.
    • A test volunteer has turned on Location sharing in My Profile.

    Steps

    1. Open Volunteers. It opens on the List; switch to Board and tap Still needed to see which departments are short.
    2. Pick two volunteers in the Unassigned pool and choose a department in the bar at the bottom. On a desktop, drag them instead.
    3. Open a volunteer's profile with (i).
    4. In Deposits, record a deposit for one volunteer, then refund it.
    5. Tap Ask location for the test volunteer. On their phone, tap Share location. Their position shows on the small map.
    6. Add a volunteer with + New User. They appear in the Unassigned pool.
    Checks to try
    • A volunteer who is only in the Volunteer department can't open the page.
    • A volunteer moved to another department loses access on the old department's key and gets Read on the new one, where that key has it.
    • A refunded deposit can't be changed or removed.
    • A location request with no answer ends after 2 hours. Decline closes it at once.
    • + New User here offers no role, no access rows and no Medical department.
    • Team Leads in the List can't be picked or moved, and have no location request or deposit.

    Venue Map: find and mark places Everyone views

    Team Leads add and edit their own department's pins and areas. Directors and Admins can edit anything and import KML files.

    Before you start

    • A Director or Admin has drawn the event's venue boundary. Team Leads can only draw inside it.

    Steps

    1. Open the map, switch between Street and Satellite, tap a pin or area, and use Directions or Street View.
    2. Add a pin: tap the map or use Use my location, then pick a type (water, first aid, toilets, stage, gate…) and your department.
    3. Pins start out locked. To move one, tap the lock button, drag the pin, then lock again (see the pin lock).
    4. Draw an area, such as a Workshop area or the Mess Hall, and give it its type.
    Checks to try
    • Scrolling the map while pins are locked moves no pins.
    • A Team Lead can't edit another department's pin or draw outside the boundary.
    • A Volunteer sees the map but has no edit or lock buttons.

    Public Page: the festival page for participants Directors, Admins, Developers

    Participants open the page at the festival's public page address, without signing in. See Public Page for everything it does.

    Before you start

    • The event is active and has its start and end dates. Only events like this can be shown.
    • Workshop sessions are planned, so Live now, Up next and the Schedule have something to show.
    • Venue Map pins and areas are in place, for "Find your way" and the map.

    Steps

    1. In Admin › Public Page, switch the page On and pick the event to show (see Settings).
    2. Set how many days before and after the festival the page shows. Before the festival it shows a countdown; afterwards, a thank-you.
    3. Add up to 3 emergency contacts (the first is the main line), up to 4 help tiles, and the "through the years" numbers.
    4. Upload photos for the highlights. Switch SOS on and pick the departments that answer it (see the next part).
    5. Tap Open page and check it on a phone: Live now, Up next, Schedule, Map, News and the Emergency button (see What participants see and The Emergency button).
    6. On the public page, star a session to add it to My plan, and allow alerts to get reminders on that phone (see My plan and festival alerts).
    Checks to try
    • Page off, or outside the days set: the page says it's resting.
    • No staff or participant names or phone numbers appear anywhere. Facilitator names are the only exception.
    • An announcement sent to the public page appears in News and the ticker. Remove from public page takes it down at once (see Public page news).
    • Install: the public page can be added to a phone's home screen on its own.

    SOS: answer a participant's call for help Directors, Admins, Developers and the SOS departments

    Before you start

    • In Admin › Public Page, the page is on, SOS is on, the SOS departments are chosen and an emergency contact is set.
    • Responders are on the event's staff list and have notifications turned on in My Profile.

    Steps

    1. On a second phone, open the festival's public page and tap Emergency › Send SOS. Allow location and add a short message.
    2. Responders get an urgent notification and a note on their Billboard.
    3. Open the SOS page. See the location on the map, then tap Directions or Call.
    4. When it's dealt with, add a short note and mark it handled.
    Checks to try
    • SOS switched off: the button disappears from the public page.
    • Privacy: SOS locations and phone numbers are deleted automatically 30 days after the event.

    Billboard: read your notes Everyone

    1. Open Billboard from the menu. The red number shows how many notes are unread.
    2. Tap a note to open it, which marks it read. Mark all read clears the rest.
    3. Tapping a phone notification opens the matching note.
    Checks to try
    • Muted notifications: the note still appears on the Billboard.
    • Private notes, such as a Team Lead's attendance reminder, appear only for that person.

    Announcements: tell the team Directors, Admins, Developers and the Helpdesk

    1. In Admin › Announcements, pick a type (General, Urgent / Safety, Schedule change, Weather, Reminder or Task). Write a title of up to 60 characters and a message of up to 240.
    2. Choose the audience: roles, departments or both. Only the event's staff is on by default; untick it to reach everyone.
    3. If you want, also post it on the Public page for participants (see Public page news).
    4. Check the Goes to line and the preview, then tap Send and confirm.
    5. In the Sent list, use Send again or Copy to form.
    Checks to try
    • No audience picked: Send stays off.
    • Urgent / Safety reaches people even if they muted announcements.
    • Task and Reminder can't go on the public page.
    • Sent notes can't be recalled from phones. A public post can be removed from the public page.

    Tasks: follow a job to the end The Helpdesk, Directors, Admins, Developers

    Before you start

    • A department with the Helpdesk key exists, with its members on the event's staff list.
    • The department that will get the task has a member with BiG on their phone and notifications on.

    Steps

    1. In Admin › Announcements, pick the Task type and send it to one department. Pick a Place from the Venue Map, such as a water station.
    2. On the receiver's phone, open the task on the Billboard and move it to Acknowledged, then In Progress.
    3. On Announcements, switch the right-hand panel to Tasks. The status and the timeline have changed, and the sender was told.
    4. Tap Show on map. The map opens on the task's place, with the open task on it.
    5. Move the task to Closed.
    Checks to try
    • Blocked asks for a reason and tells the whole Helpdesk.
    • A Team Lead or volunteer outside the department, who isn't Helpdesk, can't move the task on.
    • Reopen on a Closed task is only for the Helpdesk and Directors, Admins and Developers.
    • A WASH facility as the place selects the WASH department as the receiver. Logging Refilled there doesn't close the task.
    • The Public page card is off for a Task.
